# NutriMatch | Product Management Case Study

**Product:** Personalized Food Discovery & Nutrition Decision Support Platform  
**Role:** Product Owner / Product Manager (Independent Project)  
**Stage:** Functional prototype and early user testing  
**Focus:** Consumer HealthTech, Product Strategy, UX, Data Quality & Product Validation

## 1. Product Overview

NutriMatch is a mobile-first food discovery platform designed to help consumers evaluate how packaged foods align with their personal nutrition goals.

Rather than providing a generic health score, NutriMatch considers an individual's priorities, such as increasing protein or fibre intake, reducing added sugar or sodium, and supporting weight management.

The goal is to make nutritional information easier to understand, more relevant and actionable.

## 2. The Problem

Food labels provide extensive nutritional information, but interpreting that information can be difficult.

A product considered healthy for one consumer may not be the most suitable option for another.

**Product opportunity:** Build a simple, personalized tool that translates complex nutritional data into understandable recommendations based on individual goals.

## 3. My Role and Responsibilities

I led NutriMatch's development as an independent product project, including:

- Defining the problem, target users and product vision.
- Translating customer needs into product requirements.
- Prioritizing features and determining the minimum viable product (MVP).
- Designing the user experience and product recommendation logic.
- Integrating external food data into the product experience.
- Defining data validation rules and acceptance criteria.
- Conducting functional testing and incorporating early user feedback.
- Iterating on usability, onboarding and product explanations.

## 4. MVP Features

**Barcode Scanning:** Users can scan packaged foods to retrieve available product and nutritional information.

**Personalized Nutrition Goals:** Users can select goals such as weight management, protein or fibre intake, and reduced added sugar or sodium.

**Product Fit Recommendations:** NutriMatch provides qualitative recommendations explaining how a product aligns with selected goals.

**Portion Comparison:** Users can compare the nutritional implications of different serving sizes.

**Data Transparency:** An expandable explanation helps users understand how recommendations are generated.

**Mobile-First Experience:** The interface prioritizes accessible navigation, compact information and simple recommendations.

## 5. Key Product Decisions

### Personalization Instead of Generic Scoring

I prioritized goal-based recommendations over assigning every product a universal health score.

**Rationale:** Nutritional suitability depends on individual priorities and product context.

### Explore Before Sign-In

I selected an onboarding approach that allows users to explore the product before being required to register.

**Rationale:** Reduce friction during initial product discovery.

### Data Quality Before Recommendation

I introduced validation rules to identify inconsistent or incomplete nutrition information before generating recommendations.

These include energy-value consistency checks, serving-size validation, macronutrient checks and added-sugar ingredient cross-checks.

**Rationale:** Product trust depends on reliable source data and transparent recommendations.

## 6. Technical Collaboration and Product Validation

NutriMatch uses Open Food Facts data to retrieve packaged-food information.

I defined requirements for handling incomplete data, inconsistent values and product-category differences.

The product underwent structured functional testing, including 57 passing tests covering identified product scenarios.

Issues identified and addressed included barcode scanning failures, inconsistent energy values, ingredient-data conflicts and incorrect product identification.

## 7. Early User Testing and Feedback

More than 10 users tried NutriMatch during early testing.

Feedback identified the need for:

- Simpler explanations of product recommendations.
- Less text-heavy interfaces.
- More intuitive access to detailed nutrition information.

These findings informed improvements to content hierarchy, expandable explanations and the mobile user experience.

## 8. Product Success Metrics

Metrics identified for future structured measurement include:

- Barcode scan success rate.
- First recommendation completion rate.
- User activation rate.
- Recommendation explanation engagement.
- Repeat usage and retention.
- Frequency of incomplete-data responses.

These are proposed measures, not reported performance outcomes.

## 9. Lessons Learned

Building NutriMatch reinforced three important product lessons:

1. **Trust is a product requirement.** Data quality must be addressed before presenting personalized recommendations.
2. **Simple experiences require complex decisions.** Reducing interface complexity requires deliberate prioritization.
3. **User feedback improves assumptions.** Early feedback highlighted usability needs that were not fully apparent during initial development.

## 10. Next Steps

- Expand structured usability testing.
- Establish event-based analytics and retention measurement.
- Improve recommendation explanations.
- Evaluate user activation and repeat usage.
- Continue validating the product's value proposition with potential users.
